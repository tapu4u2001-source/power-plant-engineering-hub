LESSON 01

🌀 Turbine Fundamentals

A steam turbine is a rotating machine that converts the energy of steam into mechanical shaft power.

Boiler → High Energy Steam → Turbine → Shaft Power → Generator → Electrical Power

What actually happens?

Steam produced by the boiler enters the turbine at high pressure and temperature. As the steam expands, part of its thermal and pressure energy is converted into velocity and then mechanical work on the turbine blades.

Important idea

The turbine itself produces mechanical power. The generator converts that mechanical power into electrical power.

⚡ Practical Point

Turbine output is strongly influenced by steam flow, inlet steam conditions, exhaust pressure, turbine efficiency and governing valve position.

LESSON 02

🔥 Steam-to-Power Energy Conversion

Understanding the complete energy path makes turbine operation much easier to understand.

Fuel → Boiler → Steam → Turbine → Generator → Grid

Step-by-step

  1. Fuel releases heat in the boiler.
  2. Feedwater is converted into high-energy steam.
  3. Steam enters the turbine.
  4. Steam expands through turbine stages.
  5. The rotor develops torque.
  6. The generator converts shaft power into electricity.
  7. Exhaust steam enters the condenser.
  8. Condensate returns to the feedwater system.
Turbine Mechanical Power ≈ Steam Mass Flow × Specific Enthalpy Drop × Turbine Efficiency
LESSON 03

🏗️ Turbine Construction

A power plant steam turbine contains many mechanical and steam-flow components working together.

Main components

  • Rotor
  • Casing
  • Fixed blades / diaphragms
  • Moving blades
  • Main steam valves
  • Control / governing valves
  • Bearings
  • Lubricating oil system
  • Gland sealing system
  • Turning gear
  • Condenser
  • Protection system
🔧 Field View

When troubleshooting a turbine, always think in systems rather than looking at only one component.

LESSON 04

⚙️ HP / IP / LP Turbine Sections

Large steam turbines are commonly divided into high-pressure, intermediate-pressure and low-pressure sections.

Section Main Function Typical Condition
HP Initial expansion of high-energy steam Highest pressure
IP Further steam expansion Intermediate pressure
LP Final expansion before condenser Low pressure / high volume
💡 Important

As steam pressure falls, its specific volume increases. This is why LP turbine stages require large flow areas.

LESSON 05

🌀 Turbine Stages & Blades

Turbine expansion normally occurs across multiple stages instead of one large pressure drop.

Basic stage concept

Steam → Fixed Blades / Nozzles → Moving Blades → Next Stage

Fixed blades guide or accelerate the steam while moving blades extract energy and produce torque.

Why multiple stages?

  • Better energy extraction
  • Controlled expansion
  • Improved efficiency
  • Manageable blade speed
  • Better mechanical design
LESSON 06

🔄 Impulse vs Reaction Turbine

Two important turbine stage concepts are impulse and reaction.

Feature Impulse Reaction
Pressure drop Mainly across nozzle Across fixed and moving blades
Moving blade function Extracts momentum Extracts momentum + reaction effect
Typical concept Nozzle → Moving Blade Fixed Blade → Moving Blade
LESSON 07

🎛️ Turbine Governing System

The governing system controls steam admission so that turbine speed and load remain within the required operating range.

Speed / Load Demand → Governor → Servo System → Governing Valve → Steam Flow → Turbine Load

During synchronization

The governor primarily controls turbine speed and mechanical input before and during synchronization.

After synchronization

Once connected to the grid, increasing mechanical input generally increases generator MW output.

⚡ Remember

Governor controls the mechanical side. AVR controls the excitation/electrical voltage side.

LESSON 08

🔧 Control & Governing Valves

Steam valves determine how much steam enters the turbine.

Important valve concepts

  • Main stop valve
  • Control / governing valves
  • Valve opening feedback
  • Servo actuator
  • Hydraulic or electro-hydraulic control
  • Fast closing during trip conditions
🚨 Safety Point

Turbine valve operation is a critical protection function. Never bypass or manually defeat turbine protection/interlock systems during operation.

LESSON 09

📈 Turbine Speed & Load Control

Turbine behavior is different before and after synchronization.

Condition Main Objective
Before synchronization Control speed and reach rated frequency
During synchronization Match speed/frequency and phase
After synchronization Control mechanical input and MW load
LESSON 10

🛢️ Turbine Lubrication System

Lubricating oil protects turbine and generator bearings and provides reliable operation of rotating equipment.

Important parameters

  • Lube oil pressure
  • Lube oil temperature
  • Oil tank level
  • Oil pump status
  • Emergency oil pump availability
  • Oil filter differential pressure
  • Bearing oil supply
🚨 Critical

Low lubricating-oil pressure can result in bearing damage and may initiate a turbine trip depending on the protection logic.

LESSON 11

⚙️ Turbine Bearings

Bearings support the rotating shaft and maintain the required rotor position.

Monitor

  • Bearing metal temperature
  • Oil temperature
  • Oil pressure
  • Vibration
  • Shaft position
  • Axial displacement where applicable
🔍 Troubleshooting clue

A sudden change in vibration or shaft position should always be investigated rather than treated as a normal trend.

LESSON 12

💨 Gland Steam & Sealing System

Turbine glands control steam leakage and prevent unwanted air ingress into sections operating under vacuum.

Turbine Shaft → Gland Seals → Steam/Air Leakage Control

Why is this important?

  • Reduces steam leakage
  • Limits air ingress
  • Supports condenser vacuum
  • Protects turbine efficiency
LESSON 13

💧 Condenser & Vacuum System

The condenser converts turbine exhaust steam back into water and maintains low exhaust pressure.

Turbine Exhaust → Condenser → Condensate → Feedwater System

Why vacuum matters

Lower exhaust pressure allows greater expansion of steam through the turbine, which can improve the available enthalpy drop and turbine performance.

Common causes of poor vacuum

  • Low cooling-water flow
  • High cooling-water temperature
  • Air ingress
  • Air ejector / vacuum equipment problem
  • Condenser fouling
  • Abnormal steam or drain conditions
LESSON 14

♨️ HP & LP Feedwater Heaters

Feedwater heaters use extraction steam to heat boiler feedwater before it enters the boiler.

Turbine Extraction Steam → Feedwater Heater → Higher Feedwater Temperature → Improved Cycle Efficiency

Important parameters

  • Feedwater inlet temperature
  • Feedwater outlet temperature
  • Extraction pressure
  • Drain level
  • Heater differential pressure
  • Protection/interlocks
LESSON 15

🔀 Extraction Steam System

Extraction steam is taken from selected turbine stages and used by auxiliary parts of the steam cycle.

Typical applications

  • HP feedwater heating
  • LP feedwater heating
  • Deaerator heating
  • Process steam where applicable
💡 Practical Point

Changes in extraction conditions can affect turbine performance, feedwater temperature and overall cycle efficiency.

LESSON 16

🚰 Deaerator & Feedwater System

The feedwater system returns condensate and treated water toward the boiler while controlling temperature, pressure and dissolved-gas conditions.

Condenser → Condensate System → LP Heaters → Deaerator → Boiler Feed Pump → HP Heaters → Boiler

Watch carefully

  • Deaerator level
  • Deaerator pressure
  • Feedwater temperature
  • BFP discharge pressure
  • Feedwater flow
LESSON 17

🛡️ Turbine Protection

Turbine protection detects dangerous operating conditions and initiates appropriate protective action.

Examples

  • Overspeed
  • Low lube-oil pressure
  • High bearing temperature
  • Excessive vibration
  • Abnormal axial displacement
  • Low condenser vacuum
  • Emergency stop conditions
🚨 Important

The exact trip setpoints and voting logic must always be taken from the actual turbine manufacturer's documentation and plant protection philosophy.

LESSON 18

🚀 Turbine Startup

Turbine startup is a controlled process. The exact sequence varies by turbine design and manufacturer.

General sequence

  1. Confirm turbine auxiliaries are available.
  2. Establish required lubrication.
  3. Confirm turning gear conditions.
  4. Establish gland/sealing arrangements.
  5. Establish condenser vacuum.
  6. Prepare steam system.
  7. Admit steam according to the approved procedure.
  8. Accelerate turbine under controlled conditions.
  9. Monitor vibration and expansion.
  10. Reach rated speed.
  11. Synchronize generator.
  12. Load gradually according to operating procedure.
LESSON 19

🔄 Synchronization & Loading

Before closing the generator breaker, the generator must satisfy the required synchronization conditions.

Generator Voltage ↔ Bus Voltage
Generator Frequency ↔ Bus Frequency
Phase Sequence = Same
Phase Angle ≈ Matched

After breaker closing

Mechanical input from the turbine can then be increased to raise generator MW according to the plant operating procedure.

⚡ Remember

Synchronization connects the generator electrically. Loading the generator is a separate operating process.

LESSON 20

🚨 Turbine Trips & Immediate Response

A turbine trip rapidly removes steam input to protect the turbine and associated equipment.

When a trip occurs

  1. Confirm turbine trip indication.
  2. Identify the initiating protection.
  3. Check turbine speed.
  4. Check generator breaker status.
  5. Check steam valve status.
  6. Check lube oil pressure.
  7. Check vibration and bearing parameters.
  8. Check condenser vacuum.
  9. Review alarms and sequence-of-events data.
🚨 Never reset blindly

Do not simply reset a turbine trip without identifying and addressing the initiating condition according to the plant's approved operating procedure.

LESSON 22

📈 Vibration & Differential Expansion

Vibration and thermal expansion are important indicators of turbine mechanical condition.

Possible vibration contributors

  • Rotor imbalance
  • Misalignment
  • Bearing problems
  • Mechanical looseness
  • Steam-flow related excitation
  • Rubbing
  • Thermal effects

Differential expansion

Different turbine components can expand at different rates during startup and load changes. Monitoring differential expansion helps prevent mechanical contact or abnormal clearances.

LESSON 23

🧰 Practical Turbine Troubleshooting

Good troubleshooting starts with the symptom, trend, sequence of events and operating condition.

Example: Turbine vibration increasing

Vibration ↑ → Check trend → Check bearing parameters → Check speed/load → Check steam conditions → Check axial position → Check recent operational changes → Investigate root cause

Example: MW falling unexpectedly

  • Check governing valve position.
  • Check steam flow.
  • Check inlet steam pressure.
  • Check turbine pressure stages.
  • Check condenser vacuum.
  • Check extraction conditions.
  • Check generator electrical parameters.
🔍 Golden Rule

Do not troubleshoot from one parameter alone. Compare multiple parameters and their trends.

This case study connects turbine theory with the type of operating observations made in a real power plant.

Example Operating Condition

Parameter Example Observation

Actual Generator Load ≈ 49.8 MW
Turbine Inlet Pressure ≈ 89 kg/cm²
HP Heater 1 In Service
HP Heater 2 In Service

How an engineer should look at it

  1. Confirm generator MW from the correct measurement source.
  2. Compare turbine demand and actual load.
  3. Check governing valve position.
  4. Check steam pressure and temperature.
  5. Check steam flow.
  6. Check condenser vacuum.
  7. Check extraction/heater conditions.
  8. Compare DCS indication with field instruments.
  9. Look for abnormal trends before making adjustments.
