1. What Is Generator Synchronization?

Synchronization is the process of bringing a generator into the correct electrical relationship with a live electrical bus before closing the generator circuit breaker.

Simple definition: Synchronization means making the generator and the live bus sufficiently compatible in voltage, frequency, phase sequence and phase angle so that the breaker can be closed under the approved synchronizing scheme.
GENERATOR LIVE BUS │ │ │ │ └────────── COMPARE ───────────────┘ │ Voltage / Frequency / Phase Sequence / Angle │ ↓ SYNCHRONIZING │ ↓ SYNCH CHECK / LOGIC │ ↓ BREAKER CLOSE

The exact permissible differences and breaker-closing strategy are determined by the generator, breaker, synchronizing equipment and approved plant procedure.

2. Why Can't We Simply Close the Generator Breaker?

Before connection, the generator and the bus are two electrical sources that may not have the same instantaneous voltage relationship. Closing them when they are not sufficiently synchronized can produce large electrical and mechanical transients.

BEFORE CLOSING Generator voltage ≠ Bus voltage Generator frequency ≠ Bus frequency Phase sequence ? Phase angle ≠ 0° approximately ↓ DO NOT CLOSE ↓ Synchronizing process ↓ Check conditions ↓ Approved close

Why is this important?

  • Large current can result from an unfavorable closing condition.
  • Electrical torque can change suddenly.
  • Mechanical stress can be transferred through the shaft and coupling system.
  • Generator and system equipment can be subjected to abnormal transients.
  • Protection may operate if abnormal conditions are severe.
Safety rule: Synchronizing is a controlled switching operation. Never bypass synchronizing interlocks or the approved synchronizing-check function.

3. Generator Side vs Live Bus Side

The easiest way to understand synchronization is to imagine two electrical systems that must be compared before they are joined.

Generator sideLive bus side
Generator terminal voltageBus voltage
Generator frequencySystem/bus frequency
Generator phase sequenceBus phase sequence
Generator phase angleBus phase reference
Memory aid: We are not merely checking "generator voltage equals bus voltage." We are comparing the complete electrical relationship between the two sides.

5. Condition 1 — Voltage Matching

The generator terminal voltage should be sufficiently close to the bus voltage before closing, according to the approved synchronizing settings.

Generator voltage │ ↓ COMPARE ↑ │ Bus voltage If difference is outside the approved window: ↓ Synchronizing permission blocked

Who mainly controls generator voltage?

The AVR/excitation system controls generator excitation and therefore strongly influences generator terminal voltage.

Memory: During synchronization, think: AVR → voltage matching.
The acceptable voltage difference is plant-specific. Use the actual synchronizer/relay settings and operating procedure rather than a generic value.

6. Condition 2 — Frequency Matching

The generator frequency must be sufficiently close to the bus frequency before breaker closing.

Turbine speed ↓ Generator frequency ↓ Compare with bus frequency ↓ Synchronizing condition

Who controls generator frequency before synchronization?

The turbine governor/speed-control system controls turbine input and generator speed. For a synchronous generator, speed and electrical frequency are directly related through the number of poles.

f = (P × N) / 120

f = frequency in Hz
P = number of poles
N = speed in rpm
The frequency relationship above is for a synchronous machine. Actual speed/reference values and governor control philosophy depend on the generator and turbine design.

7. Condition 3 — Phase Sequence

Phase sequence tells us the order in which the three phase voltages reach their respective phase positions.

Example sequence: A → B → C The generator phase sequence must correspond correctly with the bus phase sequence.

Why is this so important?

If the phase sequence is incorrect, the phase relationship does not rotate in the required matching manner. This is not something that should be "fixed" by simply adjusting the governor or AVR.

Phase sequence is normally established during installation/commissioning and verified through approved testing before routine synchronization.

8. Condition 4 — Phase-Angle Matching

Even when generator and bus voltage magnitudes are close and frequencies are close, the instantaneous phase angle between the two sources must be suitable for breaker closing.

Generator phasor ↗ / θ / ────●────────────→ Bus phasor θ = phase-angle difference

As the generator frequency differs slightly from the bus frequency, the relative phase angle moves. The synchronizing equipment monitors this movement and determines the appropriate closing opportunity according to its configured logic.

Simple idea: Frequency difference makes the angle move. The synchronizer uses this relationship to determine when the closing condition is suitable.

9. Understanding the Synchroscope

A synchroscope is an instrument that indicates the phase relationship and relative frequency between the incoming generator and the live bus.

12 │ 11 │ 1 10 │ 2 9 ─────── ● ─────── 3 8 │ 4 7 │ 5 6 SYNCHROSCOPE

What does movement tell us?

The direction and speed of pointer movement indicate the relative frequency condition. The exact display convention should be confirmed from the installed synchronizing equipment manual.

Do not rely on a remembered "clock position" convention without checking the actual synchronizer. Different systems can present indications differently.

What does a slowly moving pointer mean?

It generally indicates a small frequency difference, so the phase relationship changes more slowly. This gives the synchronizing system more time to identify a suitable closing opportunity.

10. Synchronizing Lamps

Synchronizing lamps are a traditional visual method used to compare generator and bus phase relationships.

BUS GENERATOR PHASES PHASES A B C A B C │ │ │ │ │ │ └─┴─┴──── Synchronizing ─────┴─┴─┘ lamps ↓ Observe phase relationship

Different lamp arrangements indicate synchronization differently. Because the exact meaning depends on the circuit arrangement, the installed synchronizing-lamp scheme must be understood from the actual plant drawing.

Modern plants commonly use automatic synchronizing equipment, but understanding lamps is valuable for electrical fundamentals and troubleshooting.

11. What Is the AVR's Role During Synchronization?

The AVR primarily helps bring the generator voltage to the required relationship with the bus before closing.

Measure generator voltage
→
Compare with reference / bus condition
→
Adjust excitation

Important distinction

AVR → excitation / voltage.
Governor → speed / frequency.

Once the generator is connected to a strong electrical system, the interaction becomes more complex. The AVR continues to control excitation according to its operating mode, while the turbine governor controls mechanical input and active-power behavior.

12. What Is the Governor's Role During Synchronization?

Before breaker closing, the turbine must be brought to a speed corresponding closely to the required generator frequency.

Governor ↓ Steam / prime-mover input ↓ Turbine torque ↓ Rotor speed ↓ Generator frequency

The synchronizer may send speed/frequency correction commands to the governor system in an automatic arrangement, subject to the plant control philosophy.

Easy memory:
AVR = voltage matching
Governor = frequency matching
Synchronizer = compares and coordinates the closing condition

13. Slip Frequency

If generator frequency and bus frequency are not exactly equal, there is a frequency difference called slip frequency.

fslip = |fgenerator − fbus|

For example, if:

Generator = 50.05 Hz
Bus = 50.00 Hz

Slip frequency = 0.05 Hz

A small frequency difference means the phase angle changes slowly. A larger difference means the phase relationship changes faster.

The desired slip direction and closing strategy depend on the synchronizing system and plant procedure. Do not use a generic "fast or slow" rule as a substitute for the approved synchronizing settings.

14. Synchronizing-Check Relay — ANSI 25

ANSI device number 25 is commonly associated with a synchronizing or synchronism-check function. It verifies configured synchronizing conditions before permitting breaker closure.

Generator measurements ──┐ │ Bus measurements ────────┤ ↓ SYNC CHECK / 25 │ Conditions acceptable? ┌────┴────┐ YES NO │ │ ↓ ↓ Permit close Block close

Possible quantities checked

  • Voltage difference
  • Frequency difference
  • Phase-angle difference
  • Phase-sequence or associated synchronizing logic
  • Breaker status/interlocks as configured
Important: The actual ANSI 25 implementation may be inside a protection relay, synchronizing relay, generator control system or breaker-control scheme. Study the plant's actual logic diagram and relay manual.

15. What Happens When the Breaker Closes?

The breaker connects the generator electrically to the bus. From that moment, the generator is no longer an isolated source.

BEFORE CLOSE Generator ────── X ────── Bus Synchronizing conditions satisfied AFTER CLOSE Generator ────── CLOSED BREAKER ────── Bus ```

After closing, active and reactive power begin to settle according to the generator, turbine, AVR, governor and grid operating conditions.

After synchronization, watch

  • MW
  • MVAR
  • Generator voltage
  • Current
  • Power factor
  • Frequency
  • Field current
  • AVR status
  • Governor/load reference
  • Alarms and abnormal vibration/process indications as applicable

16. Manual Synchronization

In manual synchronization, the operator observes the approved synchronizing indications and makes the necessary adjustments before issuing the breaker-close command.

1. Confirm generator and bus are available for synchronization.
2. Confirm correct breaker, isolator and switching configuration.
3. Verify phase sequence and synchronizing circuit health.
4. Bring generator voltage into the approved window.
5. Bring generator frequency into the approved window.
6. Observe synchroscope / approved synchronizing indication.
7. Confirm synchronizing-check permission.
8. Close the generator breaker according to the approved procedure.
9. Confirm stable MW, MVAR, voltage, current and alarms.
Never treat this as a generic switching procedure. Actual breaker permissives, interlocks and operating steps must follow your plant's approved switching procedure.

17. Automatic Synchronization

An automatic synchronizer can continuously compare generator and bus quantities and issue control commands so that synchronization occurs within the configured operating window.

Generator V / f / phase │ ↓ AUTO SYNCHRONIZER │ ┌────┴────┐ ↓ ↓ Governor AVR correction correction │ │ └────┬────┘ ↓ Synchronizing window ↓ Breaker close

Typical automatic functions

  • Frequency/speed correction command
  • Voltage correction command where integrated
  • Phase-angle monitoring
  • Breaker closing-time compensation where provided
  • Synchronizing permission
The actual command interfaces and logic are plant-specific. Automatic synchronization does not mean that operating personnel can ignore breaker/interlock status.

18. What Happens After the Generator Is Connected?

Synchronization does not mean the generator immediately reaches its desired MW and MVAR operating point. The post-closing controls determine how loading develops.

Breaker closes
→
Generator connected
→
Controls settle
→
MW / MVAR loading

Active-power loading

Increasing turbine mechanical input generally increases active-power output, subject to governor and system conditions.

Reactive-power loading

Changing excitation changes the generator's reactive-power operating point, subject to the AVR, limiters, capability curve and grid conditions.

Connection between our masterclasses:
Synchronization gets the generator safely onto the bus.
AVR controls excitation/voltage behavior.
Governor controls mechanical input/active-power behavior.
Protection supervises abnormal conditions.

19. What Happens If Synchronization Is Wrong?

An incorrect closing condition can create severe electrical and mechanical transients. The exact consequences depend on the magnitude and nature of the mismatch.

MismatchPotential concern
Large voltage differenceHigh transient current and abnormal electrical stress
Large phase-angle differenceLarge electrical torque/transient effects
Frequency mismatchRapid phase-angle movement and unfavorable closing condition
Incorrect phase sequenceFundamentally incorrect phase relationship
Never intentionally test an out-of-phase closing condition on an operating plant unless it is part of a specifically engineered, isolated test approved by the responsible authority.

22. Practical 50 MW Turbine-Generator Case Study

Situation

A 50 MW-class turbine generator is ready to be connected to an energized bus. The operator sees generator voltage approaching bus voltage and the synchroscope moving slowly.

Step 1 — Voltage

Confirm the generator and bus voltage measurements are within the approved synchronizing window. If not, the AVR/excitation system may need attention according to the operating procedure.

Step 2 — Frequency

Compare generator frequency with bus frequency. The governor/speed-control system is used to establish the required speed/frequency relationship.

Step 3 — Phase sequence

Confirm the established phase sequence is correct. This should already have been verified through commissioning and approved testing.

Step 4 — Phase angle

Observe the synchroscope or automatic synchronizer. The closing opportunity depends on the actual synchronizer settings and breaker closing characteristics.

Step 5 — Relay 25 / synchronizing permission

Confirm the synchronizing-check logic permits closing. If it does not, investigate rather than bypassing the function.

Step 6 — Breaker close

Close the breaker according to the approved plant procedure and then immediately verify the generator's post-closing operating condition.

Learning point: The synchronization operation is not one adjustment. It is a coordinated condition involving voltage, frequency, phase sequence, phase angle, breaker timing and synchronizing logic.

23. Common Synchronization Misconceptions

MisconceptionCorrect understanding
"Matching voltage is enough."No. Frequency, phase sequence and phase angle also matter.
"AVR controls synchronization."AVR mainly controls excitation/voltage; synchronization involves several systems.
"Governor controls voltage."Governor primarily controls turbine mechanical input/speed; AVR controls excitation.
"Synchroscope only shows voltage."It indicates the phase relationship and relative frequency behavior.
"ANSI 25 is just an alarm."A synchronizing-check function can be part of the breaker-close permissive logic.
"Same frequency means synchronized."No. The phase-angle relationship must also be suitable.
"Automatic synchronization needs no operator attention."Automatic equipment still operates within configured logic and plant switching procedures.
