1. Why Does a Synchronous Generator Need Excitation?

A synchronous generator needs a magnetic field so electromagnetic energy conversion can take place. In a conventional synchronous generator, the rotor field winding carries DC current and creates the rotor magnetic field.

Simple definition: Excitation is the controlled supply of electrical power to the generator field system so that the required magnetic field is established and controlled.
TURBINE ↓ Mechanical rotation ↓ GENERATOR ROTOR ↓ Rotating magnetic field ↓ Interaction with STATOR WINDINGS ↓ 3-PHASE AC ELECTRICAL OUTPUT

Remember

  • The turbine supplies mechanical power.
  • The excitation system establishes and controls the rotor magnetic field.
  • The stator delivers the generated three-phase electrical output.
  • The governor and excitation system perform different control functions.

2. What Exactly Is Excitation?

The word excitation does not simply mean AVR. It refers to the complete system used to establish and control the generator field.

Depending on generator design, the physical arrangement can be different. Confirm the actual architecture from the generator and excitation manufacturer documentation.

Key idea: The AVR is the controller. The excitation system is the larger system through which the generator field is established and controlled.

4. Brushless Excitation System

Many large generators use a brushless excitation arrangement. In a simplified brushless system, the exciter and rectifier are mounted on the rotating shaft, allowing field current to reach the main rotor without conventional brushes and slip rings.

STATIONARY SIDE ──────────────────────────────────────── AVR / Excitation Controller │ ↓ Exciter Field │ ════════════════ ROTATING SHAFT ═══════════ │ ↓ Exciter Armature │ ↓ Rotating Diode Bridge │ ↓ Main Generator Rotor │ ↓ Magnetic Field ═══════════════════════════════════════════
  • It can avoid conventional field brushes and slip rings.
  • The main rotor field can be supplied through the rotating rectifier.
  • Maintenance requirements differ from a static excitation system.
  • Rotating diode condition becomes an important diagnostic item.
Plant-specific: Confirm the actual excitation arrangement from OEM drawings.

5. Rotating Diode Bridge

In a brushless arrangement, the exciter can produce AC which is then rectified to DC for the main rotor field.

Exciter AC ~ ~ ~ │ ↓ ┌────────────────────┐ │ ROTATING DIODES │ │ / RECTIFIER BRIDGE │ └────────────────────┘ │ ↓ DC FIELD │ ↓ MAIN GENERATOR ROTOR

Possible conditions

  • Healthy: rectification operates normally.
  • Open diode: one conduction path is lost.
  • Shorted diode: abnormal current/conduction can occur.
  • Multiple diode problems: excitation waveform and field performance can be seriously affected.
Do not diagnose from one indication alone. Confirm diode alarms with monitoring, field readings, event records and OEM-approved testing.

6. What Exactly Does the AVR Do?

AVR means Automatic Voltage Regulator. Its basic purpose is to regulate the controlled generator voltage by controlling excitation.

Generator terminal voltage ↓ PT / VT ↓ Voltage signal ↓ AVR ↓ Excitation control ↓ Exciter ↓ Main rotor field ↓ Generator magnetic field ↓ Generator terminal voltage └──────── feedback ───────→ AVR

If terminal voltage falls

Voltage ↓
→
AVR detects deviation
→
Excitation changes
→
Generator EMF changes
Remember: AVR controls excitation. It is not the turbine governor. The governor primarily controls mechanical input and active-power/frequency behavior, while excitation strongly affects generator voltage and reactive-power behavior.

7. How Does the AVR Sense Generator Voltage?

The AVR normally does not receive the generator's full terminal voltage directly. A PT/VT or another approved sensing arrangement provides a suitable measurement signal.

GENERATOR TERMINALS │ ↓ PT / VT │ ↓ Reduced measurement signal │ ↓ AVR
Conceptual example: 11,000 V generator → PT/VT → lower measurement voltage → AVR
Important: PT/VT ratios, fuses, wiring and AVR sensing ranges are plant-specific. Use actual drawings and OEM data.

Possible sensing problems

  • PT/VT circuit problem
  • Fuse or MCB issue where applicable
  • Loose/damaged wiring
  • Incorrect sensing selection
  • Calibration/configuration problem
  • Input-module fault

8. The Complete AVR Control Loop

REFERENCE / SET POINT │ ↓ ┌────────────┐ │ AVR │ └─────┬──────┘ │ ↓ EXCITER │ ↓ ROTATING RECTIFIER │ ↓ ROTOR FIELD │ ↓ GENERATOR EMF │ ↓ TERMINAL VOLTAGE │ ↓ PT / VT │ └──────────────→ AVR

This feedback loop is the central concept of the masterclass. The AVR compares measured conditions with its reference and changes excitation according to its programmed control logic.

9. Field Voltage vs Field Current

These are two different electrical quantities and should never be treated as interchangeable.

QuantityMeaning
Field voltageVoltage across the relevant field circuit
Field currentCurrent flowing through the relevant field circuit
Approximate electrical power = Field Voltage × Field Current

For a particular excitation stage with 55 V and 15.2 A:

P ≈ 55 × 15.2 = 836 W
But identify the measurement point first. A 55 V / 15.2 A reading on an exciter or controller side must not automatically be combined with a main-rotor current reading as though all measurements belong to one circuit.

10. Exciter Field vs Main Rotor Field

Different stages of the excitation system can have very different voltage and current values.

AVR-controlled exciter │ ↓ Exciter electrical output │ ↓ Rotating rectifier │ ↓ Main generator rotor field
Engineering rule: Always write down exactly where each voltage/current measurement was taken before calculating excitation power or comparing readings.

13. Over-Excitation

Increasing excitation changes the generator's electromagnetic operating point and reactive-power condition.

Excitation ↑ ↓ Generator electromagnetic operating point changes ↓ Reactive-power behavior changes ↓ Operating point moves toward the over-excited region
  • Monitor field current.
  • Monitor field voltage.
  • Monitor MVAR and PF.
  • Check OEL status.
  • Check the generator capability curve.
Never exceed approved excitation limits. Actual field-current limits and OEL settings must come from the generator/excitation OEM and approved procedures.

14. Under-Excitation

Reducing excitation moves the generator toward an under-excited operating condition. Reactive-power direction changes according to the system and sign convention.

Excitation ↓ ↓ Generator operating point changes ↓ Reactive-power behavior changes ↓ Operating point moves toward under-excited region
  • Excessive under-excitation can approach the UEL boundary.
  • Stability considerations can become important.
  • Machine heating limitations can matter depending on design.
  • UEL and other functions may act.

15. Generator Capability Curve

A generator cannot operate at every possible combination of MW and MVAR. Capability is limited by electrical, thermal, mechanical and stability constraints.

  • Stator current / heating

16. AVR AUTO / MANUAL

AUTO mode

The AVR automatically controls its regulated quantity according to its reference and configured control logic.

MANUAL mode

The operator or control system controls excitation through the excitation system's manual/field-control function, depending on equipment design.

Important: Manual mode does not automatically mean all protection is bypassed. Limiter and protection behavior depends on the actual excitation system.

Before changing mode, verify

  • Generator operating condition
  • Voltage stability
  • Field current and voltage
  • AVR alarms
  • Limiter status
  • Approved operating procedure

18. Excitation Protection

40 — Loss of Excitation

Loss-of-excitation protection detects an abnormal generator operating condition associated with inadequate or lost excitation. The exact relay characteristic and settings depend on the generator and protection study.

Rotor Earth Fault

The rotor field circuit is normally insulated from earth. An insulation failure can create a rotor earth-fault condition. Protection philosophy determines how single and subsequent faults are treated.

Over-excitation / V/Hz

Excessive magnetic flux can occur when voltage is too high for the operating frequency.

Flux is approximately related to V / f
Protection settings are not universal. Use the approved generator protection study, relay manual, OEM documentation and plant cause-and-effect logic.

19. Excitation Troubleshooting Decision Tree

GENERATOR VOLTAGE ABNORMAL │ ↓ Check AVR AUTO / MANUAL │ ┌────┴────┐ ↓ ↓ MANUAL AUTO │ │ ↓ ↓ Check manual Check AVR alarms excitation │ │ ↓ └──────→ Check voltage sensing │ ↓ PT / VT │ ↓ Field voltage │ ↓ Field current │ ↓ Exciter │ ↓ Rotating diodes │ ↓ Rotor circuit │ ↓ Find root cause
Golden rule: Do not immediately adjust excitation because voltage looks abnormal. First determine whether the problem is sensing, AVR control, exciter output, rectification, rotor field, generator operating condition or the external system.

Step 1 — Do not compare the numbers blindly

The 55 V / 15.2 A measurement and the 981.9 A measurement may belong to different electrical stages. Identify exactly where each transducer is connected.

Step 2 — Identify the architecture

Confirm whether the generator uses brushless excitation, static excitation or another arrangement. Identify the exciter, rectifier and main rotor field measurement points.

Step 3 — Build the power path

AVR → Exciter → Exciter output → Rectifier → Main rotor field → Generator magnetic field

Step 4 — Check the operating condition

Also record generator MW, MVAR, kV, PF, AVR mode, limiter status and excitation alarms before interpreting the readings.

Engineering caution: These values are educational examples. Actual expected values and limits must come from your machine's nameplate, OEM documentation and operating records.

23. Common Misconceptions

MisconceptionCorrect way to think
“AVR directly controls MW.”AVR primarily controls excitation; active-power control is principally associated with the prime mover/governor system.
“AVR directly supplies the full rotor current.”It depends on excitation architecture. In brushless systems, AVR controls the excitation system rather than directly carrying the main rotor current.
“Field voltage and field current are the same.”They are different electrical quantities.
“Exciter current must equal main rotor current.”Different excitation stages can have different voltage/current values.
“Low generator voltage always means increase excitation.”First determine the cause, sensing condition, AVR mode and operating limits.
“Manual mode means protection is disabled.”Protection and limiter behavior depends on the actual excitation system and logic.
“One MVAR value tells the whole story.”Interpret MVAR together with MW, kV, PF, field current and capability curve.
