1. 🔥 Boiler Fundamentals

A boiler converts the chemical energy of fuel into thermal energy and transfers that energy to water to produce steam.

Fuel + Air → Combustion → Heat → Water → Steam

Major Energy Conversion

The overall power-generation chain can be understood as:

Fuel → Boiler → Steam → Turbine → Generator → Electricity

Main Boiler Systems

  • Fuel preparation and feeding system
  • Combustion/furnace system
  • Water circulation system
  • Steam generation system
  • Superheater and reheater
  • Economizer
  • Air preheater
  • Flue-gas system
  • Ash handling system
  • Boiler protection and control system

2. 🔥 AFBC vs CFBC

Fluidized-bed boilers use air to suspend and mix solid particles so that combustion can take place efficiently.

Feature AFBC CFBC
Bed type Bubbling fluidized bed Circulating fluidized bed
Solids circulation Limited Continuous circulation
Particle carryover Lower Higher by design
Solids separation Usually simpler Important part of the circulation system
Fuel flexibility Good Very good
Remember: CFBC depends heavily on controlled circulation of solids through the furnace and separation/return system.

3. 🔥 Boiler Main Flow Path

Typical Fuel Path

Coal → Crusher → Feeder → Furnace

Typical Air Path

FD Fan → Air Heater → PA / SA System → Furnace

Typical Flue-Gas Path

Furnace → Cyclone / Separator → Heat Transfer Sections → Economizer → APH → ESP → ID Fan → Chimney

The exact arrangement depends on boiler design and OEM.

4. 🔥 Furnace & Combustion

The furnace is the primary combustion zone where fuel reacts with oxygen and releases heat.

Three Requirements for Combustion

  • Fuel
  • Oxygen / combustion air
  • Suitable temperature and residence time
Good Combustion = Fuel + Air + Temperature + Mixing + Residence Time

Poor mixing or insufficient residence time can result in incomplete combustion and increased CO/unburnt carbon.

5. 🔥 Primary Air — PA

Primary air is introduced through the lower portion of a fluidized-bed furnace.

Main Functions

  • Fluidization of bed material
  • Supply part of combustion air
  • Maintain proper bed mixing
  • Support fuel combustion
  • Assist solids movement
Important: PA flow must be controlled according to the boiler's design and operating procedure. Too little air can compromise fluidization; excessive air can increase solids carryover and change combustion conditions.

6. 🔥 Secondary Air — SA

Secondary air is generally introduced above the bed to support staged combustion and improve mixing.

Functions

  • Complete combustion
  • Improve fuel-air mixing
  • Support staged combustion
  • Help control CO
  • Influence furnace temperature distribution
PA → Fluidization + Primary Combustion

SA → Mixing + Combustion Completion

7. 🔥 Fuel Quality — GCV, Moisture & Ash

GCV

Gross Calorific Value indicates the heat released from a unit mass of fuel under specified conditions.

Moisture

Higher fuel moisture requires additional energy for evaporation and can affect combustion and boiler efficiency.

Ash

Ash increases the amount of non-combustible material circulating through the boiler and affects ash handling, erosion and heat-transfer surfaces.

Fuel Size

  • Ignition behaviour
  • Combustion rate
  • Residence time
  • Carryover
  • Bed behaviour

8. 🔥 Coal + Char

Coal provides fresh combustible material while char represents partially combusted carbonaceous solids.

In circulating systems, combustible solids can remain in the combustion loop for longer periods, supporting carbon burnout.

Fuel → Combustion → Hot Solids → Separation → Return → Furnace

9. 🔥 Bed Temperature

Bed temperature is a major operating parameter in fluidized-bed combustion.

It influences:

  • Combustion stability
  • Fuel burnout
  • Heat transfer
  • Ash behaviour
  • Emission performance
Engineering Note: There is no single universal bed-temperature setpoint for every AFBC or CFBC boiler. Always use the OEM operating range, alarm limits and trip settings.

10. 🔥 Furnace Draft & ID Fan

The ID fan removes flue gas from the boiler and helps establish the required furnace pressure.

More ID Fan Capacity → Greater Flue-Gas Removal → Furnace Pressure Tends More Negative

Operator Observation

  • Watch furnace pressure continuously.
  • Observe ID fan loading.
  • Check FD/PA/SA air balance.
  • Investigate sudden draft changes.
Furnace pressure limits are boiler-specific. Do not apply a generic draft setpoint to another boiler.

11. 🔥 O₂ & Excess Air

Flue-gas oxygen is an important indicator of the combustion-air condition.

Observation Possible Direction of Investigation
O₂ falling Air reduction, fuel increase or combustion imbalance
O₂ rising Fuel reduction, excess air or air leakage
High O₂ + high stack temperature Possible excess-air / heat-loss investigation
O₂ target values depend on boiler design, fuel and operating condition. Use plant-specific control limits.

12. 🔥 Cyclone & Solids Circulation

In conventional CFBC arrangements, entrained solids are separated from the flue gas and returned to the furnace.

Furnace → Cyclone → Gas Path

Cyclone Solids → Return System → Furnace

Modern CFBC designs may use different separation and recirculation arrangements. Always understand the specific boiler configuration before troubleshooting.

13. 🔥 Loop Seal / Solids Return

Where provided, a loop seal or similar return system transfers hot solids back to the furnace while maintaining the required pressure separation.

Operator should observe:

  • Fluidization air
  • Temperature
  • Pressure difference
  • Solids movement
  • Blockage or abnormal behaviour

14. 🔥 Drum & Water-Steam System

The boiler drum provides separation between water and steam in drum-type boiler circulation systems.

Important Parameters

  • Drum level
  • Drum pressure
  • Feedwater flow
  • Steam flow
  • Feedwater temperature

Three-Element Control

Drum Level + Steam Flow + Feedwater Flow

Three-element control helps maintain drum level during changing steam demand.

15. 🔥 Superheater & Reheater

Superheater

Raises saturated steam temperature above saturation temperature to produce superheated steam for the turbine.

Reheater

Steam returning from the turbine is reheated before entering subsequent turbine stages where the plant configuration includes reheating.

Boiler → Superheated Steam → HP Turbine → Reheat → IP/LP Turbine

16. 🔥 Economizer & Air Preheater

Economizer

Recovers heat from flue gas to raise feedwater temperature before the water enters the boiler.

Air Preheater

Recovers heat from flue gas and transfers it to combustion air, improving overall thermal efficiency.

Waste Heat Recovery → Lower Fuel Requirement → Better Efficiency

17. 🔥 Boiler Load Control

Boiler load is controlled by coordinating fuel input, combustion air and steam demand.

More Fuel + Correct Air → More Heat Release → More Steam Generation

A load increase should not be treated as simply increasing fuel. Air, furnace pressure, bed condition, steam temperature, drum level and other constraints must remain within their operating limits.

18. 🔥 Boiler Startup

Typical Startup Philosophy

  • Complete pre-start checks
  • Confirm water level and availability
  • Establish required ventilation/purge
  • Start required fans and auxiliaries
  • Establish fluidization where applicable
  • Start ignition/support fuel according to SOP
  • Establish stable combustion
  • Introduce main fuel progressively
  • Raise load gradually
Startup sequences are safety-critical and must follow the specific boiler OEM procedure, interlocks and plant SOP.

19. 🔥 Boiler Protection

Boiler protection systems are designed to prevent unsafe operating conditions from developing into equipment damage or personnel hazards.

Typical Protection Areas

  • Low drum level
  • High drum level
  • Furnace pressure abnormality
  • Fan trips
  • Loss of combustion
  • Fuel feeder problems
  • Flame/ignition problems where applicable
  • High temperature conditions
  • Boiler control-system failures

20. 🔥 ESP & Flue-Gas Path

After heat recovery, flue gas passes through the particulate-control system before discharge through the stack.

Boiler → Heat Recovery → ESP → ID Fan → Chimney

ESP Operator Parameters

  • Inlet temperature
  • Gas flow
  • Electrical field voltage
  • Electrical field current
  • Rapping operation
  • Hopper condition
  • Opacity / emission indication

21. 🔥 Boiler Efficiency

Boiler efficiency compares useful heat transferred to steam with the energy supplied by the fuel.

Boiler Efficiency = Useful Heat Output / Fuel Energy Input × 100

Important Losses

  • Dry flue-gas loss
  • Moisture-related losses
  • Unburnt carbon
  • Radiation and convection losses
  • Ash-related losses

22. 🔥 Practical Boiler Troubleshooting

Observation Areas to Investigate
Bed temperature falling Fuel flow, air flow, combustion, bed inventory
O₂ suddenly high Fuel reduction, excess air, air leakage
O₂ suddenly low Air reduction, fuel increase, combustion imbalance
Furnace pressure abnormal ID/FD system, dampers, gas-path restriction
Poor fluidization PA flow, air nozzles, bed condition
High CO Incomplete combustion, mixing, air/fuel balance
Steam generation falling Fuel, combustion, heat transfer, fouling
Drum level unstable Feedwater control, steam flow, level measurement
High bed DP Bed inventory, ash accumulation, solids condition
Low bed DP Low inventory, solids loss, circulation issue
These are investigation guides, not automatic diagnoses. Always verify plant instruments, trends, interlocks and OEM procedures before taking corrective action.

🎯 Final Boiler Engineer Takeaway

A good boiler engineer does not look at one parameter in isolation.

Fuel + PA + SA + Bed Condition + Draft + Temperature + O₂ + Steam Demand = Boiler Operating Condition

Always look at trends, relationships and the overall process rather than reacting to a single reading.

Golden Rule: Understand the process first, then interpret the instrument.
